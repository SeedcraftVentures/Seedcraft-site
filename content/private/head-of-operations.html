01 What this is

A studio, not a fund.

This is the bit most people get wrong in the first five minutes, so it goes first.

We start the companies

Idea, product, first users, first revenue. Seedcraft is the founder, not the funder. Everything in the portfolio was started here.

We do not write cheques

No fund, no LPs, no term sheets. We also do not raise. There is no round coming, which changes what funding actually means here.

We build with people

Every venture gets a small team who own a real share of what they build. Equity and revenue share, not day rates.

02 The thesis

Here for the Everyday Hero.

Everyone has a picture of the person they want to be. The manager whose team actually rates them. The writer who finishes things. The one who quietly stopped smoking and never made a big deal of it.

Getting there rarely comes down to trying harder. Most of the tools we are handed make the good version of the job slow and awkward, and the careless version quick. So we build the other kind. Products with the fairness and the follow through already in them, so being that person costs you nothing extra.

Every venture takes something people were already trying to do right, and makes the good version the easy one.

The only filter we apply

If an idea does not pass that sentence, it does not get built here.

03 How we think

We over index on explore.

Most studios pick one idea and spend three years exploiting it. We take the opposite bet, deliberately, because the cost of building has collapsed and the cost of being wrong for three years has not.

Explore

Take a lot of shots. Build the real thing fast, get it in front of real people, and let them tell us. Cheap to run, and every attempt teaches the next one.

  • A working product, never a deck or a maybe
  • Shared systems, so each build starts further along than the last
  • We are not trying to be right first time. We are trying to find out quickly

Exploit

Doubling down. Proving happens inside the build, not before it, so when something fits the thesis we back it properly and find out, rather than waiting for a certainty that never arrives.

  • Signal is enough to commit. Certainty is not on offer
  • A dedicated team goes around the product
  • It runs to a handover, not forever

A studio that explores widely needs far more process than one that builds one thing.

More companies, more agreements, more people, more launches, and more decisions about what to stop. That is the whole reason this role exists.

04 The model

Build it. Prove it. Hand it to the right people.

Reverse investing. Instead of paying cash for a small slice of someone else's company, we build the company ourselves and sell most of it to an operator who can scale it better than we can.

Build

Find the gap everyone walked past and build the real thing, fast. A working product, not a deck or a maybe.

Prove

Real users, real revenue, to a genuine proof point before anyone says scale. Target is a defensible valuation around £1m.

Hand over

Sell 90 percent to an operator or acquirer. Keep 10 percent equity and a revenue share. Stay close, go and start the next one.

05 Structure

Studio on top, projects underneath.

Deliberately simple. Seedcraft is the studio. Every venture sits under it as its own thing, and spins out into its own company at the point of sale.

Studio people and project people

This distinction runs through everything, including the seat I am proposing, so it is worth being explicit about.

Studio layer

Permanent. Works across every venture and never sits inside one.

  • Studio equity, moving to a salary paid from what the company generates as revenue grows
  • Seedcraft itself holds the stake in every venture, so the studio layer is exposed to all of them at once rather than betting on one

Project layer

Attached to one named venture. Built up as a product approaches launch, and transfers or dissolves at handover.

  • Project equity plus studio options
  • Cashes out when their venture sells
  • Scoped by output and responsiveness, never by hours

07 How a launch pays for itself

We bring the money forward.

We do not raise, so every launch has to fund its own first year. The mechanic is an introductory offer that pulls a big chunk of future revenue into the first thirty days, before we have spent anything on acquisition.

Worked example · Shiftly

£49,800
200 founding deals at £249
  • Sold in a defined window with a hard cap and tiered price escalation
  • Booked as prepaid multi year subscription, not a lifetime giveaway
  • Support async only and usage capped per tier, so the cost tail stays flat

Where that money goes

Team
Pays the people around that project, so equity is not the only thing on the table
Acquisition
Funds ongoing paid user acquisition, ring fenced to that product
Studio
Covers the build cost already sunk and pays us for the work
Runway
Buys the time to reach a defensible proof point without a round
Why the framing matters at exit. A pure lifetime deal looks like a hole in the accounts when a buyer runs an MRR multiple. Structured and recognised as prepaid multi year subscription revenue, the same cash supports the valuation instead of undercutting it. Getting that right across every launch is an ops job, and one I would want owned properly.
08 An opportunity to explore

Non dilutive project funding.

Something for us to consider rather than a plan I have already made. We do not raise, which normally means funding everything out of our own pocket. There is a whole layer of non dilutive money available to product companies that we have never gone after properly.

Every venture is a fresh applicant

A studio that spins out companies has a structural advantage here. Each new venture enters the system on its own, with its own eligibility, rather than one company applying over and over and running out of road.

Grants, not loans

Smaller awards from around £20k upwards, deliberately preferred over debt. Some products will not work, and none of that should be carried into the next one.

Credits as the baseline

R&D tax credits underneath everything as a standing floor, with competitive awards stacked on top. Needs R&D diaries kept properly, which is process, which is ops.

  • UK innovation funding across Innovate UK and the regional schemes, largely untouched by us so far
  • Games specific funding, where Seedcraft Games has a live product and a credible story
  • A repeatable process, not one off applications. A reusable intake and template system already exists, so an application across any project becomes a week rather than a fortnight of pain
  • Worth a proper look together before either of us decides how big a part of the role this should be

09 The seat

Head of Operations.

A studio seat, not a project one. It sits across everything and inside nothing. Four areas, in rough order of what matters first.

1 · Studio operations

The scaffolding that lets us run several things at once without dropping any of them. This is the main event.

  • Contracts across the board: contractor, service, NDA, guest co-founder
  • Option scheme, cap table hygiene, IP assignment as a policy rather than a per project decision
  • One shared view of what every project is doing this week
  • The process that makes all of that repeatable instead of remembered

2 · Talent and teams

We build teams around products, over and over. That needs to become a system rather than a series of conversations I have.

  • Recruitment for the open seats and everything after them
  • Onboarding that is the same good experience every time
  • Making sure people joining on equity feel properly looked after, because they are the whole model
  • Contribution tracking that stands up when a venture sells

3 · Funding

The non dilutive layer from the previous section, if we decide it is worth going after.

  • Own the pipeline across the ventures
  • Intake, drafting, submission and reporting as one repeatable process
  • R&D credit claims and the diaries that make them defensible
  • Deciding which venture applies for what, and when

4 · Exit readiness

Every venture is being built to be sold. Very few founders operationalise that early enough, and we are no exception yet.

  • Data room discipline from day one on each venture
  • Revenue recognition set up so launch offers help the multiple rather than dent it
  • Documented, transferable process, so a handover is a package rather than a scramble
Talent and exit readiness are not landing solely on you. Those are things we work through as a leadership team, together. They sit in the remit because someone needs to hold the thread, not because you would be doing all of it on your own.
10 The proposal

Roughly what I am thinking.

A starting position, not a final one. All of it is open, and I would rather you told me what is wrong with it than politely agreed.

Time
Up to 50 percent while this is equity based. I will not take more than half your week until I can pay you properly.
Studio equity
10 percent of the studio, vesting over 24 months with a 6 month cliff. A further 10 percent reserved, released when Seedcraft can pay you a salary and you come on full time. Up to 20 percent in total.
Launch revenue
An agreed share of the upfront launch cash on each venture. Every launch is different and there are other people relying on that money too, so the split gets set per project rather than fixed here.
Path to salary
Two triggers. Either a first exit completes, or revenue covers a salary line sustainably. Whichever comes first, we move to full time and salaried.
Commitment
Scoped by output and responsiveness, not tracked hours. We agree what you own and what lands each week. Nobody at Seedcraft logs time.
How we work
Decisions get made together. If it ever looks like I am veering off course, or something is being pushed that does not fit the thesis, jump in and say so. I would much rather have the accountability than the politeness.

I would be buzzing to have you on board.

Any questions at all, just shout. Happy to talk through any point in this, and there is plenty here I would rather work out with you than decide on my own.
